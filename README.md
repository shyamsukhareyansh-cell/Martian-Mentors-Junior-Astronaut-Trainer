## 🚀 About the Project

What would it take to survive on Mars?

**SPACEQUEST: THE LAST TRANSMISSION** is a story-driven, interactive Mars adventure that places players in the role of junior astronauts responsible for investigating and operating a mysterious Martian research outpost.

The mission begins with a cinematic emergency on Earth. The player awakens aboard the emergency pod ASTRAL-9, encounters the onboard AI ECHO, and follows a mysterious transmission that leads to Mars.

At the outpost, the player explores different rooms, investigates equipment, collects evidence, solves interconnected puzzles and uncovers the mystery of the missing crew.

The experience combines science, exploration, problem-solving and storytelling to make learning about space missions interactive.

## 🎯 The Challenge We Address

SPACEQUEST was developed in alignment with NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026's **Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer** challenge.

The challenge focuses on helping young learners understand the challenges of operating a lunar or Martian outpost, including:

- Life-support systems
- Power generation and allocation
- Water management
- Food production
- Radiation protection
- Resource management and decision-making

SPACEQUEST uses a Martian research outpost as its central environment and introduces these concepts through exploration, puzzles and survival decisions.

## 🎮 Gameplay and Features

### 1. Cinematic Opening

The experience begins with a cinematic sequence depicting an emergency on Earth, followed by the player's awakening aboard ASTRAL-9.

### 2. Eleven Explorable Rooms

Players explore the Martian outpost through interconnected locations:

1. Arrival Airlock
2. Central Hub
3. Power Station
4. Life Support & Water Facility
5. Botanical Dome
6. Radiation Shelter
7. Rover Bay
8. Research Laboratory
9. Mission Operations
10. Restricted Crew Archive
11. Subsurface Annex

Each location contributes to the larger investigation and mission progression.

### 3. Interactive Puzzles and Investigation

Players inspect objects, collect evidence, solve keypads and choice-based puzzles, reconstruct events and connect clues across different rooms.

The investigation gradually reveals the mystery surrounding the missing crew.

### 4. ECHO and ZYRA

Two characters accompany the player:

- **ECHO:** The mission AI, providing operational information, instructions and guidance.
- **ZYRA:** A mysterious companion who communicates through transmissions and provides clues.

The characters use synthetic voices and dialogue to support the narrative experience.

### 5. Riddle and Equation Vault

An optional training activity includes:

- 25 space-related riddles.
- 15 applied science and mathematics challenges.

Players can practise scientific reasoning and problem-solving through these activities.

### 6. Dust-Storm Resource Allocation

Players face a dust-storm scenario in which limited resources must be allocated across essential outpost systems.

A learning debrief explains the consequences and trade-offs associated with their decisions.

### 7. NASA OSDR RadLab Reference Activity

The Radiation Shelter includes an optional activity referencing NASA's Open Science Data Repository (OSDR) RadLab.

It uses a historical International Space Station radiation-data sample as a learning reference.

These measurements are from low Earth orbit, not Mars-surface measurements or predictions of Mars exposure.

## 🔬 Science and Learning

SPACEQUEST aims to make scientific knowledge useful for completing the mission.

Players encounter concepts related to:

- Energy and power distribution
- Oxygen and life support
- Water recovery
- Plant growth and food production
- Radiation shielding
- Mathematical reasoning
- Scientific observation and evidence-based decisions

The project distinguishes between simplified gameplay values, concepts based on real science and values directly supported by cited sources.

The scientific references and integration details are documented in the `docs/` directory.

## 🧪 Playtesting

The game was tested through repeated browser playthroughs, including testing with two children.

The playtesting helped the team assess the gameplay experience, navigation, puzzle interactions and educational engagement.

The project is designed for children aged 7–12, with the aim of making space science approachable through interactive exploration.
